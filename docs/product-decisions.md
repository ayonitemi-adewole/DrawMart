# Product decisions (as of 28 Oct 2026, store listing flow updated same day)

Working name: DrawMart (placeholder)

## Decided
- **Launch market:** Lagos only, under an LSLGA public online lottery licence. Expand state by state; restrict ticket purchases by state in the code from day one.
- **Separate apps:** Buyer, store and admin are separate apps, entered only through one shared sign-in page that sends each account to its own app. Each account belongs to exactly one app; store and admin accounts can't buy tickets. Sign-ups are buyers; DrawMart sets up store accounts after verification. Browsing draws stays public.
- **Regular products:** Stores list each item as a raffle draw or a regular product. Regular products get an admin review, then go straight on sale at a fixed price; no tickets, escrow or caution fee, and the item stays with the store. The store pays the platform fee (10–30%) out of the sale.
- **Buy now:** Anything on sale at a fixed price (regular products, and draws that ended unsold) is bought from the wallet, store credit first. Fixed-price buys don't count towards the weekly ticket-spending limit. The buyer gives a Lagos delivery address and phone; the store marks the order sent; the buyer confirms delivery, and only then is the store due its payout. One unit per listing.
- **Messages:** Buyers can message the seller (store or individual) of any regular product from its page; one chat per buyer per product, readable only by the two of them. No chat on raffles: DrawMart holds and checks those items. Sellers can opt in to a call number that buyers see only inside a chat with them. Messages that look like a request to pay outside DrawMart (an account number, "transfer to my Opay") get a warning for the reader. Chats refresh every few seconds while open.
- **Seller insights:** Stores (Insights in the store app) and individual sellers (Sell → Insights) see, for the last 7 or 30 days: views, different visitors, buyer chats, tickets or items sold, sales in naira, and the share of visitors who bought, in total, per day and per listing, with a plain-language tip per live listing. A view is one visitor opening a listing page, once per visitor per listing per Lagos day; signed-in visitors count by account, signed-out ones by a random browser id. The seller's own visits don't count. Ticket sales on refunded draws don't count. Sellers never see who viewed.
- **Boosts (paid promotion):** Stores and individual sellers can pay to show a live draw or an item on sale in the explore page's Sponsored banner (an automatic carousel under the hero, one boosted item at a time, every 5 seconds) and first on the home page and in its category, marked "Sponsored". Fixed packages: 3 days ₦2,500, 7 days ₦5,000, 14 days ₦9,000 (placeholders, in `public.boost_packages`). Paid by card/bank through Flutterwave and started only after the server verifies the payment; a new boost on an already-boosted listing runs on after the current one. Boosts unlock once 1,000 buyers have been active in the last 30 days (bought a ticket or item, or topped up by card/bank); an admin can change that number or open boosts early. At most 12 run at once app-wide, and each list shows at most 4 sponsored items at the top. Not refunded if the item sells or the draw ends early. Sellers see when a boost payment is still pending, when a boost is active, and its results: views, people, chats, units sold and sales during the boost against the same number of days just before it.
- **Explore and browsing:** The explore page groups live draws and buy-now items by category, each in one row that scrolls sideways (at most 4 cards in view on large screens: 3 whole and three-quarters of the next, so it's clear the row scrolls; 2 + ¾ on tablets, 1 + ¾ on phones), with an "All …" link to `/browse`, which shows everything for a category, a seller or a search, 15 items a page.
- **Related items:** Under every listing: a scrolling row of more items from the same seller ("All from …" opens that seller's items), then a scrolling row of similar items from other sellers ("All …" opens the category). Same layout as the explore page.
- **Sellers:** Raffle draws: registered stores and companies only (CAC verified, admin-approved). Regular products: stores, and individuals who pass an ID check.
- **Individual sellers:** Any buyer can apply under **Sell** in the buyer app with their legal name, NIN, phone, home address, a photo of a government ID and a selfie holding it. An admin compares them against a checklist and approves (opening a shop) or sends it back with a reason. Individuals keep their buyer account, sell regular products only (each listing still reviewed), can't buy their own items, and get paid after the buyer confirms delivery. ID photos sit in a private bucket only the applicant and admins can open.
- **Escrow:** DrawMart collects the item from the store (the store books a pickup day and address) and verifies it in escrow before tickets go on sale. Seller is paid after it's delivered to the winner.
- **Cars:** Too big to store, so they skip escrow. The store books an on-site inspection (car, VIN, papers, photos) instead, and the car stays with the dealer until the winner collects it.
- **Caution fee:** Paid by the store when it sends a listing for review. Refunded with the payout if the draw sells out; kept to cover pickup/return or the inspection visit if it doesn't sell out or is rejected.
- **Shared pickups:** A store can send 2 to 10 draw items in one pickup and pay one caution fee, the highest among the items. Each item keeps its own listing, admin review and draw. The whole fee is refunded only once every draw in the pickup has sold out, and kept as soon as any one ends another way (unsold, kept on sale, or rejected). Cars can't share a pickup, because each is inspected where it is; regular products have no pickup or fee.
- **Pinned locations:** Delivery checkout and pickup/inspection booking use a live map: the buyer or store pins the exact spot (drag the map, search, or use their location) and gives a written address. A pin inside Lagos is required on new orders and bookings. The pin is as private as the address: buyer, store and admins for orders; store and admins for bookings. Stores and admins get a directions link. Uses OpenStreetMap maps and Nominatim lookups, so the map area and pinned point are sent to OpenStreetMap's servers.
- **Cart and checkout:** Buyers keep a cart of regular products and draws they've won, and check out everything together: paid from the wallet, store credit first, all or nothing. Won items are free, because the tickets paid for them. Cars never go in the cart: each is arranged on its own and delivered from the dealer. The cart holds nothing back; an item someone else buys first shows as no longer available. "Buy now" uses the same checkout for one item.
- **Pickup locations:** At checkout, buyers choose delivery (with a map pin) or collection from a DrawMart pickup location (`public.pickup_points`, placeholder addresses for now). Cars can't be collected from one.
- **Who sends won items:** DrawMart, from escrow, listed for admins under Won items; the store doesn't see the winner's details. Won cars stay with the dealer, so the store sends those.
- **Saved pickups:** While listing draw items, a store can continue to pickup details, save and add another item, or save and come back later to add more or book the pickup. One saved pickup per store; no listing exists and no fee is paid until it's sent. It lasts 7 days from the first save (`app_settings.saved_pickup_days` and `RULES.savedPickupDays`); edits don't extend it, and afterwards the items are removed. Cars can't be saved, because they can't share a pickup.
- **Accounts and saved addresses:** Each buyer has an account page with their name, phone and state (state is set by identity checks once test mode is off), plus settings. They can save up to 10 delivery addresses, each with a name, map pin and rider's phone, and one default. Checkout starts on the default, and a new address can be saved there. Orders keep their own copy of the address, so changing a saved one never changes an order already placed.
- **Delivery confirmation:** Buyers can't mark anything as received. Delivery accounts (riders, drivers and pickup-counter staff, in their own app at `/delivery`) do it, using a 6-digit PIN only the buyer can see, shown from the moment they check out. The buyer gives the PIN once they have the item, so nobody can claim a delivery that didn't happen. Each item in a checkout has its own PIN. Five wrong tries pauses that order's PIN for 30 minutes, which also applies if another rider takes over. An order the seller has marked ready goes in a shared pool; any rider can take it and only then sees the buyer's name, address, map pin and phone, and loses them on handing it back or delivering it.
- **Product IDs:** Every product gets a short, permanent ID when it's submitted, like PRD-7K3Q9X. It leaves out characters that are easy to mix up (0, O, 1, I, L), and nobody can choose or change it. Sellers, admins and buyers can all find a product by its ID, whole or in part, on every page that lists several products. Buyers can see it, so it can be quoted in chats and on delivery labels. Explore and Browse only find what's live or on sale; admins can find anything under All products.
- **Categories:** No perishable goods (Food removed), since items can sit in escrow for weeks.
- **Deadlines:** The store picks the draw length, 3–60 days, counted from approval.
- **Unsold draws:** The store picks one of: refund buyers; extend once by 7 days, then refund; or refund buyers and keep the item on DrawMart as a regular product at a fixed price the store sets when listing.
- **Fees:** DrawMart sets the platform fee from the item's value; sellers (stores and individuals) never choose the rate and the form doesn't show it, only the naira fee and their payout. Under ₦50,000: 7.5%; ₦50,000–₦499,999: 5%; ₦500,000–₦4,999,999: 3%; ₦5,000,000 and up: 2.5% (the floor). Raffles are rated on the item price, products on the sale price. On raffles the store still picks who carries the fee: store pays (launch default, so buyers see one clean ticket price) / buyer pays / split. Products: the seller always pays it.
- **Payments:** Users top up a wallet; tickets are bought from the wallet to avoid gateway fees on small tickets. Store credit is spent before cash.
- **Top-up provider:** Flutterwave (card, bank transfer, USSD), test keys only until the licence is granted. A top-up is ₦100 to ₦500,000. The wallet is credited only after our server verifies the payment with Flutterwave.
- **Draws:** Provably fair. Publish SHA-256 of a secret seed before sales; winner = SHA-256(seed:last-ticket-ref) mod total + 1; reveal seed with the result. Stream draws live.
- **Announcing the winner:** Not at sell-out. When the last ticket sells, sales close and every participant is notified. The winner is announced on the listing's deadline, or 7 days after the last ticket if that's sooner (`announcementDate`, `RULES.maxDrawWaitDays`). The outcome is fixed at sell-out, because the seed and closing reference are both set, but stays secret until the announcement. Sold-out draws show on the Results page with a countdown. The caution fee is refunded and consolation credit paid when the winner is announced. Notifications are in-app for now. A winner is also greeted with confetti and a pop-up showing the prize the next time they open the app, once per win across devices. It waits during checkout, and seeing the result page first counts as having seen it.
- **Consolation:** Losing tickets return a share (default 20%) of spend as store credit.
- **Responsible gaming:** 18+ KYC, max 20 tickets per person per draw, user-set weekly spending limit (lowering instant, raising waits 24h).
- **No real money** until the licence is granted; run in test mode. Test mode has shared-password test accounts (6 buyers, one an ID-verified seller, and 5 stores) and an admin "Reset test data" button; delete the test accounts before launch.
- **Tech:** React + TypeScript + Tailwind v4 (Vite), React Router, TanStack Query. UI talks only to a `DrawMartApi` interface. **Backend: Supabase** (Postgres + Auth + Storage), with all writes as database functions; the in-browser mock stays for demos.
- **Saved items:** A heart on every listing card and listing page saves a draw or product to the buyer's Saved list, under Account. Anything buyers can see can be saved, including finished draws; an item that's later taken down drops off the list. The list is private to that buyer: stores and admins can't read it, and sellers aren't told. Saving sends nothing and creates no reminders. A signed-out tap goes to sign-in, then saves. No sharing or public wishlists. Alerts about saved items come later (backlog item 8) and will follow the backlog's limits.
- **Plain numbers on draws:** Every live draw card shows the ticket price, the chance per ticket ("1 in 520"), the share sold and the time left. The listing page adds the buyer's own chance for the tickets they've chosen, counting any they already hold. The chance is always tickets held out of all tickets, because a draw only runs once every ticket has sold. Share sold rounds down, so a draw with tickets left never reads 100%. `src/domain/chance.ts`.
- **Browse filters:** `/browse` filters by ticket price (up to ₦500, ₦1,000 or ₦5,000, including that price), closing today (Lagos time), almost sold out (80% or more) and new this week (listed in the last 7 days). Sorts are closing soonest, cheapest ticket and most sold (by share of tickets, not number). Filters combine with category, seller and search, and live in the address. Ticket, closing and sold-out options only apply to live draws, so they leave out buy-now items; new this week applies to both. Paid boosts stay on top whatever the sort, as on the explore page. `src/domain/browse.ts`.
- **Practice draw:** `/practice` lets anyone play one pretend draw, signed in or not: pick up to 12 of 20 pretend tickets, watch the rest sell, and see the result and how consolation credit works. It runs the real draw maths from `src/domain/draw.ts` in the browser only, is labelled "Practice: no money" on every step, and never touches the wallet or ledger. There's no prize and no credit, so it can't be mistaken for a free entry. The result is stated plainly, with no spinning reveal and nothing about how close it came. The home page offers it to anyone without tickets until they try it or close the offer (remembered on that device). How it works links to it.
- **Sharing and link previews:** Every listing page has a "Share to WhatsApp" button beside the phone's own share button. The message states the price and doesn't promise a win. A shared link shows chat apps the photo, title, ticket price and share sold, from two Vercel functions in `api/`; `vercel.json` sends only known chat-app and social crawlers there, and people get the app as usual. The functions read with the public key, so a listing buyers can't see gets the general DrawMart preview. Previews can be up to 10 minutes old on Vercel, and chat apps keep their own copies for longer.
- **Winners wall:** `/winners` is public and shows winners who chose to appear: first name only, what they won, the date, and a link to check the draw. It's opt-in after a win, with a consent box, and the prize never depends on it. The first name is one word with no digits, so no full names or phone numbers can be entered. Nothing else about the person is collected. Admins approve each one, and sharing again sends it back for review. A winner can withdraw at any time from the result page or Account: it comes off the wall and the name is deleted. What was agreed and when is kept in a private log with no name in it. No client can read `winner_stories`, because it ties an account to a win. There are no sample winners: the wall stays empty, and the home page strip hidden, until real ones appear. Photos and videos aren't included for now. `src/domain/winnerStories.ts`.
- **Push notifications:** Web push, on phones and computers, for wins, draw results, orders and messages, delivered within about a minute even with the app closed. Buyers are only asked after a meaningful moment (buying a ticket, or from their tickets page), never on a first visit, and the browser's question only appears after they tap Turn on. Each kind can be switched off in Account. What shows on a lock screen is deliberately limited: never a delivery PIN, never the words of a message, and nothing about a draw's result before it's announced. Signing out stops notifications on that device, so the next person on a shared phone doesn't get them. Store and admin apps don't have notifications yet.
- **Home-screen app:** The manifest's display is `standalone`, decided so that iPhones can get notifications, since they only allow it for apps added to the home screen. The catch: email sign-in links open in the browser rather than the installed app, so those buyers sign in again inside it. Password sign-in is unaffected.
- **Saved-item alerts:** Buyers hear about things they've saved: a draw passing 90% sold, a draw within 24 hours of closing, a store listing an ended item again, an item going on sale, or an item getting cheaper. In the app and by push. They're rationed because they can nudge someone to buy: once per saved item per kind, at most 3 a day per buyer (Lagos time, and any over that are dropped, not saved up), and none about draws once a buyer's weekly limit has no room for a ticket (the limit doesn't cover items on sale, so those alerts still go). One switch, on the Saved page and in Account, turns them all off. The wording states facts, with no countdowns or "hurry". There's no relist action, so a relist means a new listing with the same title from the same store after the first ended. Sellers can't edit prices yet, so the lower-price alert is ready but won't fire from the app until they can. `src/domain/savedAlerts.ts`.
- **Draw reveal:** When a draw's winner is announced, everyone who held a ticket sees a short reveal ending on the real winning ticket the next time they have the app open, once per draw across devices. Winners go on to the win pop-up with confetti. Others get the plain result and how consolation credit works, with no confetti. After time away, only the latest loss is shown. The roll is decoration and the result is already fixed. To avoid near-misses, which encourage problem gambling, it never shows the viewer's own tickets or the numbers beside them, never shows the winner early, keeps a steady pace instead of slowing to a stop, and is skipped for very small draws or when the device asks for reduced motion. The result page keeps a Replay button. It can be switched off on the Notifications page. `src/domain/reveal.ts`.
- **Early access:** A new draw opens to regular buyers an hour before everyone else, with an "Early access" badge and its opening time public on the listing. A regular has at least 3 completed purchases in the last 90 days: delivered orders, plus finished draws they were in. Each counts once however many tickets it took, and a won prize's order isn't counted on top of its draw, so this doesn't reward heavy play. Regulars keep every other limit, including the weekly limit and 20 tickets per draw. Regular products have no early access. In test mode, an admin can open a draw to everyone straight away from Live draws. The hour is `app_settings.early_access_minutes`.
- **Languages:** The app is moving to message files (`src/i18n/`), Nigerian Pidgin first, then Yoruba, Hausa and Igbo. A buyer's choice is saved on their account, so it follows them to a new device, and on the device for when they're signed out. No language is chosen automatically: English is the default. A language only reaches real buyers once a native speaker has checked it (`reviewed` in `src/i18n/languages.ts`); until then it shows in test mode only. Regulated wording (draw rules, refunds, responsible gaming, licensing) lives under `regulated` and stays in English until reviewed, so a mistranslation can't change what a buyer is told about their money. A test enforces this. Moving screens over happens one at a time; anything not moved yet stays in English.

## Open
- Pidgin needs a native speaker's check before real buyers see it, and then the regulated wording needs translating and checking too.
- Winners wall consent wording (`WINNER_CONSENT_TEXT`): have the lawyer check it against the Nigeria Data Protection Act, and how long the consent log is kept.
- Caution fee amounts. Placeholders in `src/domain/listing.ts`: ₦2,500 phones/fashion, ₦7,500 electronics, ₦10,000 home, ₦25,000 cars.
- Whether the caution fee should also be kept when admin rejects a listing before pickup happens (currently: kept in every non-sell-out case).
- Order problems: cancellations, refunds if a store can't deliver, and disputes (not built yet; admins handle them by hand).
- Automatic NIN verification with NIMC through a KYC provider (Dojah, Prembly, Smile ID), instead of only an admin's eyes. Also: how long to keep ID photos and NINs after review (NDPA 2023), and whether to encrypt the NIN column.
- Limits for new individual sellers (e.g. price cap or number of live items until they have good sales), and removing a seller's shop.
- Messages: instant delivery (Supabase Realtime) and email/push notifications for new messages; letting users report or block someone, and whether admins can read a chat when a report comes in; in-app voice calls instead of a phone number.
- Insights: signed-out views can be inflated by someone clearing their browser or scripting visits; add bot filtering or rate limits per IP before sellers rely on the numbers (or before paid promotion, idea 6). How long to keep raw view rows. Where a view came from (search, home page, shared link).
- Boosts: final package prices; whether a boost should pause or refund when its listing sells out early; whether paid placement needs approval under the LSLGA licence (it promotes lottery draws, not just products); a waiting list when all slots are taken.
- Delivery: whether riders should see a rough area before taking a job, or admins should assign jobs instead of a shared pool; how riders reach a store to collect (only the store's name and area are shown today, because a store's phone is opt-in); what happens if a buyer can't be reached or refuses delivery; rider pay.
- Map provider for launch: OpenStreetMap's public map and lookup servers are fine for testing but their usage policy rules out heavy use; move to a paid or self-hosted provider (Mapbox, MapTiler, Google) before launch. Also check the privacy notice covers sending locations to that provider (NDPA 2023). Delivery fees by distance could use the pins later.
- Notifications beyond the app: email, SMS or push when a draw sells out or a winner is announced. Today people only see it when they open the app.
- Real pickup locations and hours, and who moves store-held regular products to them (today the store marks the order as sent to the location). How long an item waits there before it's returned.
- Whether a winner has a deadline to arrange a won item.
- Stock: one unit per listing today. Should regular products have a quantity?
- "Skip the draw: buy now" on a live raffle (the button still says "coming next").
- Whether selling regular fixed-price items affects the LSLGA licence terms; check with the gaming lawyer.
- Draw fairness: the closing reference is generated by our server; take it from a public randomness source (e.g. drand) before launch.
- Store payouts and caution-fee charging: Flutterwave transfers too, or another provider.
- Whether ₦500,000 is the right single top-up cap, and whether to add a daily deposit cap for responsible gaming.
- Final name and brand.
- The fee tier rates and boundaries above are placeholders (edit `public.fee_tiers` and `FEE_TIERS` in `src/domain/fees.ts` together).

## Legal context
- Since the Supreme Court ruling of 22 Nov 2024, lotteries are regulated by each state, not federally (the FCT is separate). The National Lottery Act 2005 was struck down.
- A ticket-for-a-prize-by-chance model counts as a lottery under Lagos rules, so it needs an LSLGA licence. The "promotional raffle" category excludes standalone, continuous gaming businesses.
- Several northern states restrict gambling; plan expansion state by state.
- Get advice from a Nigerian gaming lawyer before launch.
